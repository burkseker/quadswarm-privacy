Quadswarm Privacy Policy

Effective date: 3 November 2026 (Google Play's rating card added; what stays on the device brought up to date)

Quadswarm ("the game") is a top-down swarm game published by Burak Seker ("we"). This policy explains what the game does with data. It is short because the game itself does very little with it: it is played entirely on your device, asks for no account and asks you for nothing. What goes online is the advertising and, now and then, Google Play's own rating card, both described below.

What we collect

Nothing. We have no server and receive no data from the game. We cannot see who plays it, what settings you choose, or how you do.

What stays on your device

The game keeps the following in its own private storage on the device, and never sends any of it anywhere:

  • your settings: the language, the colour theme and your own colour, the difficulty, and whether swarm marks, auto zoom, sound and vibration are on;
  • your record: how many matches you have played, how many you have won, the biggest swarm you have led and how many swarms you have broken;
  • your progress towards the achievements;
  • when the game last asked Google Play for its rating card, so that it does not ask again within a month.

Clearing it is up to you: Settings → Reset record in the game clears the record, Android Settings → Apps → Quadswarm → Storage → Clear data resets everything, and uninstalling the game leaves nothing behind.

Advertising

The game shows a full-screen advertisement as it is opened and as each new match starts, and, only when you choose to watch one, a rewarded advertisement that gives a broken swarm a second chance. The advertisements are served by Google AdMob, which is part of Google. To serve them, the AdMob software inside the game collects and processes data about the device, including your device's advertising identifier, IP address, approximate location derived from that address, device and operating system details, and your interactions with the advertisements.

This processing is Google's, not ours; we receive only aggregate counts of how many advertisements were shown. How Google uses this data is described in:

  • Google's Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

Backup advertising (Unity Ads)

When Google has no advertisement to show as a match starts, the same break may be filled by Unity Ads, a service of Unity Technologies. To show, measure and protect advertisements against fraud, the Unity Ads software inside the game may collect data similar to the above (IP address and the approximate location derived from it, device and app details, your interactions with the advertisements, and the advertising identifier) and share it with Unity. Unity Ads is only started once Google has failed to provide an advertisement. How Unity uses this data is described in:

  • Unity's Game Player and App User Privacy Policy: https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy

Your advertising identifier

You can reset or delete your advertising identifier at any time in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads, which stops advertisements being tied to the previous identifier.

Ratings

After some of your wins, at most once a month, the game may ask Google Play to show its rating card. The card belongs to Google Play: Google Play decides whether it appears, and any stars or review you give go to Google Play, which publishes them on the game's store page with your Google Play profile. The game never sees what you write, and nothing is sent unless you submit a rating. Google's Privacy Policy covers it: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Consent, if you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland

Before the game requests a single advertisement, it shows you a consent form served through Google's User Messaging Platform, a Google-certified consent management platform. Nothing is requested until you have answered it, and your answer decides whether the advertisements you see are personalised.

You can change that answer whenever you like: the form is reachable again from Settings → Privacy. That row appears only where the form was shown in the first place. Unity Ads follows the answer you give in this form; if the form did not ask about Unity, Unity shows its own consent page before its first advertisement.

Children

The game is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect anything from them.

Permissions

The game requests internet access, network state, the advertising identifier and Android's ad services permissions, along with the wake lock and foreground service permissions that Google's libraries use to finish their background work. All of them come from the advertising software and exist only so an advertisement can be fetched and counted. The game asks for no camera, microphone, location, contacts, files or storage. Vibration feedback goes through Android's haptic feedback and needs no permission.

Changes
